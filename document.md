# CourtLOG Monetization & Institutional Sales Plan

**Prepared:** 1 November 2026<br>
**Status:** Commercial strategy proposal—not a price list, approved procurement plan, or claim that CourtLOG is ready for live deployment.

## 1. Executive recommendation

CourtLOG should pursue an **institution-paid model for courts**: a recurring licence priced by court/site or court cluster, supported by one-time implementation and training fees, with optional managed hosting, support, and approved integrations.

The court institution is the customer and payer. Court staff are users; litigants, counsel, and the public may benefit indirectly, but should not be charged to access the court's administrative workflow. CourtLOG should not sell case data, charge per case/hearing/outcome, or market the current synthetic AI demo as a real predictive service.

This model creates a path to recurring revenue while keeping pricing predictable for institutional budgets. It also matches the existing project recommendation to sell organization-wide rather than to individual staff, and to price by site or institution rather than by case activity.

## 2. Target audience and buying group

### Primary target customer

**A court institution with authority over registry operations, technology procurement, and the relevant budget.** Depending on the jurisdiction, this may be a judiciary or court administration organization. The exact contracting authority and procurement route must be confirmed for each prospective customer; do not assume that one court official can authorize a purchase on behalf of an entire institution.

### Buying and approval group

| Group | Role in a potential purchase |
|---|---|
| Court administration / registry leadership | Sponsors the problem definition, operating workflow, and pilot scope. Chief Registrar or equivalent registry leadership may be an operational champion; this does not by itself establish purchasing authority. |
| Court ICT / security / data-protection staff | Review hosting, integration, access control, data handling, incident response, and technical fit. |
| Finance and procurement | Confirm budget ownership, procurement route, pricing format, contract term, and payment process. |
| Legal and policy leads | Review workflow rules, forms, data-sharing terms, and any jurisdiction-specific requirements. |
| Court staff | Filing, registry, courtroom, administrative, and custody roles that would use the accepted workflows. |
| Public and court users | Indirect beneficiaries of better administrative visibility; they are not the proposed paying customer. |

### Buyer, user, and beneficiary distinction

- **Buyer/payer:** the authorized court institution or its authorized public-sector procurement body.
- **Users:** appropriately assigned court staff. The current role model includes registry and court operations roles; access is still governed by court/division assignment.
- **Beneficiaries:** court staff and people affected by court administration. Do not make them responsible for paying a usage toll.

## 3. Value proposition to validate

CourtLOG's commercial case should be based on administrative usefulness, not on unverified claims about judicial outcomes. Potential value areas to test with a court institution are:

- More visible physical case-file custody and handovers.
- Easier tracking of selected registry, hearing, review, and execution events.
- Clearer role-scoped views and an auditable history of supported actions.
- Less staff time spent locating files or assembling routine operational status information.
- A configurable, complementary workflow tool—not a replacement for e-filing, a court case-management system, or an approved legal process unless a specific integration and policy approval are completed.

Before making a return-on-investment claim, measure a baseline and compare it with an authorized pilot: for example, file-retrieval time, unresolved custody exceptions, time spent compiling operational reports, workflow completion, staff adoption, system availability, and support effort. Use only data and measurement methods the court has authorized.

## 4. Recommended revenue streams

### A. Synthetic demonstration — lead generation, not the product being sold

Offer a local or controlled demonstration using fictional records to help an institution understand the interface and discuss requirements. Keep it clearly labelled as a demo. Do not use real case data, connect it to a live court environment, or imply that the synthetic risk or duration estimates have real-world predictive validity.

A free demo can reduce sales friction; it should not include open-ended custom development or production support.

### B. Paid discovery and scoped pilot

Once the relevant readiness and approval gates are met, offer a fixed-scope engagement for one court/site or another deliberately limited institutional boundary. The fee can cover workflow discovery, deployment configuration, training, a defined support period, and agreed evaluation. Consider crediting some or all of the pilot fee toward a subsequent annual contract if the institution proceeds.

The pilot agreement should state:

- The exact site, roles, workflows, duration, success measures, exclusions, and support contacts.
- Whether data is synthetic, anonymized, or specifically authorized live data. No real records or outcomes may be used without a documented permission reference and applicable safeguards.
- Who owns and controls the records; who may access them; retention, return/deletion, incident, and exit arrangements.
- That the pilot is not a legal approval, production certification, or commitment to a wider rollout.

If CourtLOG is not ready for an operational pilot, sell only a bounded discovery/design engagement or use grant/sponsor funding for readiness work; do not describe a live-data deployment as ready.

### C. One-time implementation, onboarding, and training

Charge separately for customer-specific work such as:

- Configuration and workflow mapping within approved policy.
- Environment setup, account/role onboarding, and administrator training.
- Staff training and supported rollout.
- Authorized data migration or import, only after written permission and a tested migration plan.
- Deployment documentation and acceptance support.

Define deliverables and limits in advance. Custom requests should use a written change order so that implementation work does not become unpriced, unbounded support.

### D. Annual institutional software licence

Use a predictable recurring fee, primarily based on **number of courts/sites or an institution-wide band**. A limited number of user bands can be considered if they reflect genuine service costs, but charging each necessary staff member separately risks discouraging adoption.

A standard annual licence could cover the accepted software scope, routine updates, and a defined level of standard support. Higher service levels, hosting, or integrations should be separate contract options rather than hidden costs.

### E. Optional managed hosting and operations

Where approved by the institution, offer a recurring managed-service fee for the environment and operations, such as:

- Hosting and persistent storage.
- Backups and tested restore support.
- Monitoring, maintenance, and version upgrades.
- Incident handling and agreed support hours or service levels.
- Security and operational reporting appropriate to the deployment.

Offer institution-controlled hosting or a private deployment option only after the architecture and support process can reliably meet the buyer's requirements. Hosting estimates in the repository are staging comparisons, not commercial price quotes.

### F. Approved integrations and custom development

Charge a separately scoped project fee for integrations or custom workflows after the institution approves the interface, data-sharing basis, security design, and acceptance criteria. Ongoing support for an integration can be included in a recurring service tier or separately contracted.

CourtLOG does not currently establish live court-system/e-filing integration or verified live WhatsApp delivery. Do not bill for or claim an operational integration until it is implemented, authorized, tested, and accepted. Provider or messaging fees, if later applicable, should be disclosed and passed through transparently.

### G. Grants, innovation funding, or donor sponsorship

Use grants, innovation programmes, or an authorized sponsor to fund early discovery, readiness work, or a first controlled pilot when a court budget is not yet available. Treat this as a bridge to a sustainable institutional contract—not the only long-term revenue plan. Any funded work must still have clear data permissions and procurement/ownership arrangements.

### H. Future analytics or AI module — only after separate validation

A future institution-paid analytics option may be considered only after the court authorizes the necessary data use, real-world outcomes are properly defined, and independent evaluation and human oversight are in place. The current classifier and duration experiment use synthetic data. The duration demo estimates days to a simulated next-progress event, not time to case conclusion, and cannot support a real-world accuracy claim or an AI surcharge today.

## 5. Packaging suggestion

| Offer | Commercial structure | Appropriate scope |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic demo | Free or sponsor-funded, with a small fictional dataset | Product evaluation only; no live data, integrations, or production SLA. |
| Discovery / readiness engagement | Fixed project fee | Requirements, authorized workflow mapping, deployment design, cost estimate, and pilot plan. |
| Single-site pilot | Fixed fee for a bounded period and scope | Supported evaluation only after readiness, data, policy, security, and site approvals. |
| Court/site annual licence | Annual recurring institutional contract | Accepted core workflows for one court/site or a defined site band. |
| Multi-site / institution contract | Annual enterprise or jurisdiction-wide contract | Multiple sites, central administration, agreed support levels, and only those integrations that have been delivered and accepted. |
| Managed operations and professional services | Recurring service fee and/or fixed project fee | Hosting, backups, support, training, approved migration, integrations, or scoped customization. |
| Validated analytics add-on | Future recurring option, subject to approval | Only after real-world evidence and governance gates are satisfied. Not available as a claim about the current synthetic demo. |

The tiers should differ mainly by deployment scale, support, hosting, resilience, and approved integrations—not by making an essential operational role unavailable to staff who need it.

## 6. Pricing and profitability method

Do not publish a Naira or dollar price until CourtLOG has buyer interviews, a defined pilot scope, and measured delivery costs. A simple contract structure is:

> **First-year contract value** = one-time implementation/training + annual site/institution licence + selected managed-service tier + separately approved integration work.

> **Renewal value** = annual licence + selected managed-service/support tier + any recurring integration or provider costs agreed in the contract.

Build the annual price from the cost to serve each institution: hosting and storage, backup/restore, monitoring, security work, support hours, upgrades, customer success, third-party costs, and an allocation for ongoing product maintenance. Track gross margin per customer and support hours per site; do not assume that a low cloud bill means the service is profitable. Price implementation separately so that onboarding does not erode recurring margins.

Profitability depends on repeatable delivery. Standardize deployment and training, define a supported configuration, cap included custom work, charge for approved changes, and offer volume pricing only where the support and infrastructure economics justify it. Multi-year or multi-site discounts should not reduce the contract below the cost and risk of serving that customer.

Validate prices with the actual institutional buyer and procurement team. Confirm whether the institution expects a purchase order, invoice, annual contract, or another authorized mechanism; do not assume that consumer credit-card subscriptions fit public-sector purchasing.

## 7. Technical and commercial prerequisites

The monetization plan is **not implemented in the application today**. In particular, `COURTLOG_PAID_FEATURES=court_clerk` is a deployment-level feature toggle. It does not charge, verify payment, create a subscription, or support separate Court Clerk purchases by organization. There is no selected payment provider or billing lifecycle.

Before selling differentiated plans within a shared deployment, implement and test organization-aware entitlements, including at minimum:

- Customer/organization and licensed-site identity.
- Feature entitlement, status, effective dates, renewal/expiry, and any agreed account or site limit.
- Server-side enforcement that remains separate from role, court, and division authorization.
- A verified, auditable contract/invoice or payment update process, plus suspension, renewal, cancellation, and dispute handling.
- Clear tenant separation and data export/exit procedures.

For institutional sales, a contract/invoice or purchase-order workflow may be more appropriate than card billing; select the mechanism with the buyer and its procurement authority. Do not treat a manual environment variable as proof that payment has been collected.

## 8. Trust, data, and pricing guardrails

- The court institution retains control of its records. CourtLOG revenue must come from software and services, not sale, advertising use, or unauthorized reuse of court data.
- Do not charge litigants, counsel, or individual staff per case, filing, hearing, message, or outcome. Avoid pricing that could create a barrier to access or a disincentive to record activity.
- Do not monetize unvalidated predictions or claim legal decision support. Keep AI optional and non-consequential until independently validated and authorized.
- Keep simulated notifications, synthetic data, and non-operational integrations clearly labelled.
- Preserve canonical identifiers in authorized data; synthetic examples must not copy real identifiers, titles, or outcomes.
- Legal rules, forms, and workflow thresholds require the appropriate Law Lead or institutional approval; a commercial contract does not substitute for that approval.
- Define data ownership, permitted processing, access, retention, security responsibilities, incident notice, backup, export, and deletion in the customer agreement.

## 9. Go-to-market sequence

1. **Confirm the buyer.** Identify the court institution, budget owner, operational sponsor, ICT/security reviewers, and procurement route. Validate the problem and willingness to pay before setting prices.
2. **Rebaseline readiness.** The repository's status note (dated 14 October 2026) describes CourtLOG as a prototype, not pilot-ready or production-certified. The completion plan listed 21 October 2026 as a pilot target; record the current status and a new approved schedule before making delivery commitments.
3. **Choose a narrow initial scope.** Prefer a small registry/custody workflow that can be accepted without operational AI or unconfigured external integrations. Specify exclusions and an honest fallback for each unavailable service.
4. **Close approval and delivery gates.** Obtain workflow/policy approval, written data permission if using real records, privacy/security review, staging controls, backup/restore, device/browser acceptance, support ownership, and staff training.
5. **Run a paid or sponsored limited pilot.** Measure baseline and post-pilot operational indicators, total support effort, adoption, reliability, and customer satisfaction. Do not treat synthetic model metrics as pilot outcomes.
6. **Convert to an annual institutional contract.** Use the accepted scope, cost-to-serve evidence, procurement feedback, and a defined service level to set the renewal price.
7. **Expand by site and capability.** Add courts, managed services, integrations, or future validated analytics only through approved scope and repeatable delivery.

## 10. Decisions to resolve before publishing a price list

- Which court institution and jurisdiction is the first target, and who has authority to contract?
- Which administrative workflow is sufficiently complete and valuable for a limited paid pilot?
- Will the institution require institution-hosted, private-hosted, or vendor-managed hosting?
- What support hours, uptime, backup, restore, incident, and response commitments can CourtLOG actually meet?
- What are the measured implementation, hosting, support, security, and ongoing maintenance costs per site?
- Which procurement, invoicing, tax, data-protection, and contract terms must be reviewed by qualified advisers?
- What non-AI operational outcomes will demonstrate value to the buyer?

## Summary

The recommended model is **court-institution-funded software and services**: a fixed-scope discovery or pilot, one-time onboarding and training, a recurring per-court/site or institutional licence, and optional managed operations and approved integrations. Grants may fund early validation. Validated analytics may be a later add-on, but the synthetic AI demo is not a commercial prediction product. Keep pricing predictable, protect court data, and base profitability on repeatable implementation and support—not per-case charges or access fees for individual court users.
